# HydroFlux — Narrative, sound, and the language of touch

Contre-Jour taught with silhouettes and one finger. HydroFlux teaches with water that carries light. The player never grabs the drop. They shape the world, and the water decides.

The cavern is dark. Light exists only inside the water. Move the water, and you move the only thing you can see.

## How a chapter grows

Each chapter adds one verb. The room shows it before any sentence does. Level 0 may whisper a line. Later levels stay quiet.

| Chapter | Name | New verb | What the player feels |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | The First Drop | Membrane | A single light trusts a vine. Pull back, let go, the vine throws it. |
| 1 | The Vein | Timing | Two vines. The drop is already falling. You load one while the other rests. |
| 2 | The Thread | Split | One drop becomes a short string of drops. A gate counts them. |
| 3 | The Breath | Current | Air moves water without touching it. A vent, then a slow draft. |
| 4 | The Cup | Volume | The goal is no longer one drop. A bowl must fill. Drops merge into a pool. |
| 5 | The Dark | Absence | The background glow dies. Only the water is visible. Miss, and the room disappears. |
| 6 | Ember | Heat | Fire does not attack. It dries. A pool shrinks. Steam can lift what a vine cannot. |
| 7 | The Fall | Flow | A waterfall. You guide a sheet of water, not a marble. |

Chapters 0–5 are the first game. Ember and The Fall are the second breath: the same touch, a larger body of water, and heat as the opposite of staying wet.

## Level 0, in the room

A stalactite forms one light. It settles on a black vine. Across a stone tooth, a ring waits. The only sentence on screen changes with the moment:

- forming: “A light gathers in the dark.”
- leaving the tooth: “It lets go of the tooth.”
- seated: “It waits in the curve.”
- still waiting: “It looks for your hand.”
- while pulling: “The vine holds its breath.”
- in the air: “It is given to the air.”
- on a surface: “It opens where it lands.” Then, if the face is level, “It finds the low place.” and “It is one drop again.” On a wall, “It spills, slowly.” On a slope, “It follows the slope.”
- missed: “The dark takes it back.”
- landed: “The well is lit.”

## Touch, not buttons

| Control | Hand | Water |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Membrane | Drag and release | Shelf, sling, and pocket. The drop sits on the upper side and leaves only when the vine snaps. |
| Vent | Tap, or hold | A breath. Short tap flicks. A hold is a draft that bends a fall. |
| Sponge | Press | Drinks the drop, then weeps it out slowly when you lift. |
| Valve | Tap once | Opens a throat. Stays open until the water has passed. |
| Cup | None | The goal. It wants volume, not aim. |
| Shade | Drag a stone | Blocks a draft, or later blocks heat. |
| Channel | Drag a lip | Turns a flow without throwing it. |

Later obstacles are the same verbs in a meaner room: a membrane that is already taut, a vent you must not hold too long, a sponge that leaks if you wait, a valve that closes on its own, heat that steals a pool you were saving.

## Sound

The bed is a spa inside a cave, not a soundtrack with a melody.

- Far: a low room tone, soft and uneven, like air in a wet throat.
- Mid: irregular drips, each with a different pitch, never on a grid.
- Near: the drop forming (a tiny rising tick), letting go (a single knock), landing in the vine (a muted tap), the vine stretching (a wet fiber, pitch rising with tension), the snap (a short cello-pluck, not a cartoon boing), the well (a glass note that rings longer than the splash).

When water becomes a thread, drips overlap into a whisper. A cup filling adds a rising tone, the pitch following the waterline. A waterfall is noise with a slow pulse, not a loop that restarts.

Silence is a tool. Chapter 5 drops the bed. You hear only the drop you still have.

## Fire, later

Fire is the loss of water, not a weapon.

- A coal under a basin lowers the waterline.
- A drop that crosses heat becomes steam: it rises, ignores the vine, and condenses on a cold tooth back into a drop.
- A membrane near heat dries, shortens, and will not hold until water touches it again.
- Sound: a soft crackle under the drip, then a hiss when steam is born. The spa tone thins, as if the air got hotter.

The player’s answer is still water. You do not put the fire out with a button. You bring the light back to where it can condense.

## Large water

A single drop is chapter 0. A flow is many drops that agree.

- A thread: 4–8 drops with a shared pace.
- A pool: drops that touch become one body. The body has a surface. A membrane can lift the surface, not each marble.
- A fall: a sheet. You drag a lip and the sheet bends. The well fills by volume. Overflow is a second path, not a failure, unless the room says the overflow is lost to heat.

We will not simulate a real fluid on a phone. The player should believe it is water because of merge, sheen, sound, and the way a pool keeps a level.

## What stays true

- Black shapes, light behind them, water as the brightest thing.
- One new verb per chapter.
- No puzzle text after Level 0, except a line when the player has already failed once.
- Mobile hands: the grab is the whole vine, not a tiny handle.
